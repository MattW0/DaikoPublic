> [!info] Description
> - Area north-east of the [[Nations/Daicon Kingdom/Redfen/Redfen (Remilitarized Zone)|Redfen (Remilitarized Zone)]] and the [[Nations/Daicon Kingdom/Daicon Kingdom|Daicon Kingdom]]
> - On the east-north lie the territories of the [[Nations/Ironreach/Ironreach|Ironreach]]
> - Mainly ruled by elves but welcoming to everybody. *"Momou darfsch gärn cho, mir si eifach besser aus du."*
> - Stretches to the far east and is built on weak-ish alliances between the elven cities

![[_assets/Sprites/Maps/region_freelands.webp|649x486]]

In the freelands, there is no single ruler. Instead, the region survives through a web of old treaties, mutual-defense pacts, marriages, trade agreements, and occasionally thinly veiled threats. The elves dominate politically and culturally, but dwarves, humans, halflings, and other peoples are common in the larger cities.

The Concord is the foundation of modern Freeland politics, though its members have repeatedly come close to abandoning it. That became particularly important again after the [[Nations/Daicon Kingdom/Redfen/Redfen (Remilitarized Zone)|Redfen (Remilitarized Zone)]] were established as a de-militarized frontier. [[Nations/The Freelands/Towns/Tarrowen/Tarrowen|Tarrowen]] grew into the primary eastern gateway between the elven lands and the contested borderlands and acted as the trade hub between elves and humans.

The siege of Tarrowen is exposing the fundamental weakness of the Freelands: everyone agrees that the cities should remain independent and free of human control, but nobody agrees on how much they should sacrifice for one another. Tarrowen desperately needs food, soldiers, and medicine. Letharis has supplies but hesitates. Sylvarra wants war. Vaelwyn wants diplomacy. Elarion wants the trade routes reopened. Meanwhile, Daicon forces continue tightening the siege, betting that hunger will accomplish what their armies cannot.

> [!tldf] Cities
> - [[Nations/The Freelands/Towns/Tarrowen/Tarrowen|Tarrowen]]: The largest trading city in the region and one of the few places where non-elves actually outnumber elves. Merchants from across Daiko gather here, making it politically neutral and extremely wealthy.
> - Thalmyr: A hill-city built across steep green ridges. Known for terraced farms, windmills, and old stone watchtowers overlooking the lowlands. Its elves are practical and somewhat isolationist, believing the southern cities have brought the current troubles upon themselves. Thalmyr controls several important mountain passes and could become crucial if the siege of Tarrowen spreads north.
> - Sylvarra: A smaller fortified city built around an enormous ancient tree. Its population is heavily militarized and it has sent the largest volunteer force toward Tarrowen.
> - Letharis: Wealthy northern city built around enormous ancient trees. Famous for luxury goods, magical craftsmanship, and its painfully arrogant nobility. It has the resources to help Tarrowen but is reluctant to commit openly.
> - Vaelwyn: A sprawling woodland city known for its druids, hunters, and strong connection to the old elven traditions. Its people are suspicious of both Daiko and the increasingly militarized politics of the Freelands.
> - Eryn Vael: A highland city surrounding a massive natural amphitheatre of cliffs and pine forests. The city is famous for its archers, falconers, and skilled stoneworkers. It maintains a small but well-trained militia and claims neutrality in the conflict, though its council is divided between those wanting to aid Tarrowen and those fearing Daicon retaliation. Beneath the city lie ancient elven tunnels that supposedly connect to forgotten settlements further north.
